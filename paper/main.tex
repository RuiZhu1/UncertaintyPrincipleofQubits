\begin{abstract}
Making one quantum measurement more predictable can force another to become
less so.
Majorization expresses this trade-off with a single probability vector, valid
for every state, that bounds a whole family of uncertainty measures at once.
We study the simplest case: two measurements with two outcomes each.
The optimal such vectors are known; we prove that no state attains them unless
the measurements commute.
Every bound they imply for a strictly Schur-concave measure, such as the
Shannon entropy, is therefore strict; only the R\'enyi entropies of order zero
and infinity are bounded tightly, and the direct-sum form fails at large
orders.
The Shannon bound still beats the Maassen--Uffink relation unless the
measurements are nearly mutually unbiased, and becomes tight in ratio, though
slowly, as they approach commuting ones.
We also derive the optimal bound for noisy and biased measurements, show that
detector bias, unlike detector noise, can invalidate ideal-detector uncertainty
relations and the steering tests built on them, and extend the results to any
finite dimension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ===========================================================================

The uncertainty principle is one of the defining features of quantum
mechanics~\cite{Heisenberg1927,Robertson1929,BuschHeinonenLahti2007}: there are
pairs of measurements whose outcomes cannot both be predicted with certainty,
however carefully the system is prepared.
This preparation uncertainty is distinct from measurement uncertainty, which
limits how accurately incompatible observables can be measured
jointly~\cite{BuschLahtiWerner2014}; we are concerned with the former.
First formulated for the position and momentum of a particle, the principle
has since become a quantitative tool of quantum information science.
It underlies the security of quantum key
distribution~\cite{BB84,Berta2010,TomamichelRenner2011}, provides
experimentally accessible criteria for
entanglement~\cite{HofmannTakeuchi2003,Guhne2004,Berta2010} and for
Einstein--Podolsky--Rosen steering~\cite{Walborn2011,Schneeloch2013,Uola2020},
and is closely
related to, though distinct from, the incompatibility of quantum
measurements~\cite{HMZ2016,Guhne2023}.
These applications call for formulations that apply to measurements with a
finite number of outcomes and whose bounds do not depend on the state of the
system.

Robertson's variance-based relation~\cite{Robertson1929} does not meet these
requirements: its lower bound depends on the state, it can vanish even for
incompatible observables, and variances are not natural when the outcomes are
mere labels~\cite{Deutsch1983}.
Entropies of the outcome distributions provide a
remedy~\cite{Hirschman1957,Beckner1975,BBM1975,Deutsch1983,Kraus1987}.
The best known entropic uncertainty relation, due to Maassen and
Uffink~\cite{MaassenUffink1988}, bounds the sum of the Shannon entropies of the
outcomes of two measurements by a quantity that depends only on the largest
overlap between their eigenvectors.
Entropic uncertainty relations have since grown into a broad field with
applications throughout quantum information
theory~\cite{WehnerWinter2010,Coles2017}; they have been refined in various
regimes~\cite{deVicente2008,ColesPiani2014} and tested in
experiments~\cite{Prevedel2011,LiXu2011}.

Each such relation, however, constrains a single measure of uncertainty, while
different tasks call for different measures: the Shannon entropy quantifies
average information, the min-entropy governs guessing
probabilities~\cite{KRS2009}, the R\'enyi family interpolates between these
and other extremes, and the Tsallis family generalizes the Shannon entropy in a
different way.
Majorization~\cite{MarshallOlkin2011}, the order that captures when one
probability distribution is more disordered than another, makes it possible to
treat all of them at once.
It is a central tool of quantum information theory, where it governs, for
instance, the transformations of entangled states~\cite{Nielsen1999} and,
more generally, quantum resource
theories~\cite{Gour2015,ChitambarGour2019}.
In the context of uncertainty, the idea is to find a single probability vector,
independent of the state, that majorizes the outcome statistics of every state;
every uncertainty measure that respects majorization then obeys a bound
determined by this one vector. Depending on whether the two outcome
distributions are combined by a direct sum or by a tensor product, such measures
include the Shannon and Tsallis entropies or the Shannon and all R\'enyi
entropies, among many others.
This program was initiated by Partovi~\cite{Partovi2011} and developed into
universal uncertainty relations by Friedland, Gheorghiu and Gour~\cite{FGG2013}
and by Pucha{\l}a, Rudnicki and {\.Z}yczkowski~\cite{PRZ2013}; Rudnicki
\emph{et al.} subsequently introduced a direct-sum formulation that yields
stronger entropic bounds~\cite{RPZ2014}.
Majorization uncertainty relations have since been extended to generalized
measurements, mixed states and quantum
operations~\cite{RasteginZyczkowski2016,Baek2019,Puchala2018},
sharpened further~\cite{Li2016,LiQiao2019,XiaoFangGour2019,Yuan2023}, in
particular by exploiting the lattice structure of majorization, verified
experimentally~\cite{Wang2020,Yuan2023}, and turned into steering
witnesses~\cite{YangQiao2026}.

The price of this universality is only partly understood.
For a qubit measured in two bases---the setting of many experiments and, for
mutually unbiased bases, of the BB84 protocol~\cite{BB84}---entropy-specific
relations can be optimized: the optimal Shannon relation is
known~\cite{SanchezRuiz1998,Ghirardi2003}, in closed form over part of the
range and through a one-parameter minimization otherwise, and so are optimal
relations for R\'enyi
entropies~\cite{BosykPortesiPlastino2012,ZozorBosykPortesi2013}.
Earlier majorization bounds have been compared with the Maassen--Uffink
relation and with the optimal Shannon bound~\cite{KTW2014,Baek2019}, and the
optimal direct-sum bound for two qubit bases has been identified in the
majorization lattice~\cite{LiQiao2019}. Partovi already remarked that the
optimal majorization vector is in general not the statistics of any state, so
that a minimum-uncertainty state need not exist in this
framework~\cite{Partovi2011}, a point recently reiterated in
Ref.~\cite{Conlon2026}. Li and Qiao also observed that the
partial sums of this bound are usually reached by different states and that, in
examples, its Shannon entropy falls short of the optimal Shannon
bound~\cite{LiQiao2018}.
What is missing is a complete account of when such bounds are tight, why, and
how this depends on the uncertainty measure. In the simplest setting, where
both approaches can be carried out completely, what exactly does majorization
gain, and what does it cost?

In this paper we answer this question for pairs of binary (two-outcome)
measurements, and the answer is simple to state.
Unless the measurements commute, no state attains the optimal majorization
bound, so every bound it implies for a strictly Schur-concave measure of
uncertainty, such as the Shannon entropy, is strict; only the R\'enyi entropies
of order zero and infinity are bounded tightly.
The price is substantial: for two qubit bases the direct-sum Shannon bound
falls short of the exact minimum by up to $22\%$. Even so, it outperforms the
Maassen--Uffink relation unless the measurement axes on the Bloch sphere are
within about nine degrees of orthogonal, i.e., unless the measurements are
nearly mutually unbiased~\cite{Baek2019}, and its ratio to the exact minimum
tends to one, though only logarithmically, as the measurements approach
compatibility, where the Maassen--Uffink relation is weakest. The optimal bound
for noisy and biased measurements also has a practical consequence: detector
noise can only increase uncertainty, but detector bias, produced for instance by
mismatched efficiencies or click/no-click detection, can push the statistics
outside the ideal quantum set. A single inequality decides when relations
derived for ideal detectors survive; when it fails, an uncertainty-based
steering test can certify steering for a product state. For the Shannon
entropy alone, a known closed-form bound~\cite{SanchezRuiz1998,KTW2014} is
stronger than both at every angle; the case for majorization rests on its
universality, not on its sharpness for any single measure.

Several ingredients of this analysis are known.
For two qubit bases the relevant majorization bounds are special cases of the
general constructions of Refs.~\cite{PRZ2013,FGG2013,RPZ2014}; the optimality of
the direct-sum bound and the states that saturate its partial sums were found
in Ref.~\cite{LiQiao2019}; the expectation values that qubit states can reach
are known to fill an ellipse~\cite{LiQiao2015,KTW2014,Abbott2016}; and for
unbiased unsharp qubit observables the direct-sum bound was evaluated in
Ref.~\cite{Baek2019}; the optimality of the flattened direct-sum vector for
general measurements was shown in Ref.~\cite{Yuan2023}; and the relation
between the overlap and the anticommutator of two binary observables in any
dimension is due to Kaniewski \emph{et al.}~\cite{KTW2014}.
We give a unified and self-contained treatment that derives both bounds, their
optimality and the states that saturate each of their partial sums from this
ellipse.
What is new is a proof of the non-attainment result for both bounds, which
makes rigorous, for binary measurements, the general remark of
Partovi~\cite{Partovi2011} and the observations of Ref.~\cite{LiQiao2018} on
the direct-sum bound, and its consequence, the strictness of all induced bounds for
strictly Schur-concave measures, with only R\'enyi orders zero and infinity
tight; a quantitative account of the resulting gap; the comparison of both types
of majorization bound with the Maassen--Uffink relation and with the exact
optimum across the whole R\'enyi family, including an explicit counterexample to
the direct-sum form at large orders, whose failure for $\alpha>1$ was stated
without proof in Ref.~\cite{RPZ2014}; the optimal direct-sum bound
for arbitrary noisy and biased two-outcome qubit measurements, the conditions
under which it is attained, and its relation to joint measurability, including
a trade-off of classical origin that bias creates between commuting
measurements; a criterion for when ideal-detector uncertainty relations survive
imperfect detection, with its consequence for steering tests; and the extension
to binary measurements in any finite dimension, with both bounds for projective measurements and the direct-sum
bound for noisy and biased ones.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} introduces majorization, the uncertainty measures and
the measurements we consider, and Sec.~\ref{sec:geometry} describes the
geometry of the achievable statistics.
Section~\ref{sec:projective} derives the optimal bounds for projective qubit
measurements, and Sec.~\ref{sec:entropic} discusses their entropic consequences
and compares them with entropy-specific relations.
Section~\ref{sec:povm} treats general two-outcome qubit measurements, which
may be noisy or biased and are described by positive operator-valued measures
(POVMs), i.e., sets of positive operators that sum to the identity, and the
consequences of detector imperfections; Sec.~\ref{sec:dimd} treats binary measurements in arbitrary dimension.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the results, including their relation
to quantum optics, and concludes.
All analytical results were also checked numerically
(Appendix~\ref{app:numerics}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Majorization and uncertainty measures}

For $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ let $x^\downarrow$ denote its entries in non-increasing
order and $L_k(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k x^\downarrow_i$ its Lorenz partial sums, with
$L_0(x)=0$; the Lorenz curve of $x$ is the piecewise-linear interpolation of the
points $(k,L_k(x))$, $k=0,\dots,n$.
For $x,y$ with equal total, $x$ is \emph{majorized} by $y$, written $x\maj y$,
if $L_k(x)\le L_k(y)$ for all $k$~\cite{MarshallOlkin2011}.
A function $\Phi$ is Schur-concave if $x\maj y$ implies $\Phi(x)\ge\Phi(y)$,
and \emph{strictly} Schur-concave if, in addition, $\Phi(x)>\Phi(y)$ whenever
$x\maj y$ and $x^\downarrow\neq y^\downarrow$.
Every function $\Phi(x)=\sum_i\phi(x_i)$ with $\phi$ (strictly) concave is
(strictly) Schur-concave~\cite{MarshallOlkin2011}.

The uncertainty measures used below are the R\'enyi~\cite{Renyi1961} and
Tsallis~\cite{Tsallis1988} entropies
\begin{equation}
  H_\alpha(p)=\frac{\log_2\sum_ip_i^\alpha}{1-\alpha},\qquad
  T_\gamma(p)=\frac{1-\sum_ip_i^\gamma}{\gamma-1},
  \label{eq:entropies}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha\in[0,\infty]$ and $\gamma>0$; the cases $\alpha\in\{0,1,\infty\}$ and
$\gamma=1$ are defined by continuity, with the Shannon entropy $H=H_1$ as the
common limit ($T_\gamma\to H\ln2$ as $\gamma\to1$). The orders $\alpha=\infty$ and
$\alpha=\frac12$ give the min-entropy $H_\infty(p)=-\log_2\max_ip_i$ and the
max-entropy $H_{1/2}$~\cite{KRS2009}, and $H_0$ is the logarithm of the number of
non-zero entries.
All of them are Schur-concave; $H_\alpha$ is strictly Schur-concave for
$0<\alpha<\infty$ and $T_\gamma$ for all $\gamma>0$, whereas $H_0$ and $H_\infty$
are not.

Let $p(\rho)$ and $q(\rho)$ denote the outcome distributions of two
measurements $M$ and $N$ on a state $\rho$; we also write $H_\alpha(M)=H_\alpha(p)$
and $H_\alpha(N)=H_\alpha(q)$. For measurements in orthonormal
bases $\{\ket{m_i}\}$ and $\{\ket{n_j}\}$, the Maassen--Uffink (MU)
relation~\cite{MaassenUffink1988} reads
\begin{equation}
  H(p)+H(q)\;\geq\;-\log_2 c,\qquad c=\max_{i,j}|\langle m_i|n_j\rangle|^2 .
  \label{eq:MU}
\end{equation}
It constrains one particular functional of the pair $(p,q)$; a different
uncertainty measure requires a different relation. A majorization uncertainty
relation~\cite{Partovi2011,FGG2013,PRZ2013,RPZ2014} instead consists of a
single state-independent vector $\omega$ such that
\begin{equation}
  p(\rho)\oplus q(\rho)\;\maj\;\omega
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  p(\rho)\otimes q(\rho)\;\maj\;\omega
  \qquad\forall\rho .
  \label{eq:maj-UR}
\end{equation}
We call these the direct-sum and the tensor-product form, respectively. Every
Schur-concave function $\Phi$ then inherits the bound
$\Phi(p\oplus q)\ge\Phi(\omega)$ or $\Phi(p\otimes q)\ge\Phi(\omega)$.
Sums of Shannon or Tsallis entropies are Schur-concave functions of $p\oplus q$,
since, the totals of $p$ and $q$ being fixed, they are sums of a concave
function over all entries; sums of Shannon or R\'enyi entropies are
Schur-concave functions of $p\otimes q$, since
$H_\alpha(p\otimes q)=H_\alpha(p)+H_\alpha(q)$. The direct-sum form also yields
bounds for R\'enyi entropies of order $\alpha\le1$, by a separate argument
(Corollary~\ref{cor:direct}), but not for all orders
(Proposition~\ref{prop:compare}).

Restricted to non-negative, non-increasing vectors of fixed length and fixed
total, $\maj$ is a partial order, and in fact a complete
lattice~\cite{CicaleseVaccaro2002,Bosyk2019}.

\begin{definition}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a set of non-negative vectors of common length and total.
A vector $\omega$ is an
\emph{optimal majorization bound} for $\mathcal{A}$ if $a\maj\omega$ for all
$a\in\mathcal{A}$, and $\omega\maj\omega'$ for every $\omega'$ with this
property; i.e., $\omega=\sup\mathcal{A}$ in the majorization lattice.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[\cite{CicaleseVaccaro2002,Bosyk2019}]
\label{lem:sup}
Let $S_k=\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}L_k(a)$. Then $\sup\mathcal{A}$ is the vector whose
Lorenz curve is the least concave majorant of $k\mapsto S_k$ (with $S_0=0$). In
particular, if $\omega_k=S_k-S_{k-1}$ is already non-increasing, then
$\omega=\sup\mathcal{A}$.
\end{lemma}

In all cases treated below the second, simpler statement applies.
The quantities $S_k$ have a transparent meaning: $S_k$ is the largest value that
any state can give to a sum of $k$ entries, i.e., to the summed probabilities of
$k$ outcomes.

\subsection{Two-outcome qubit measurements}

A qubit state is $\rho=\tfrac12(\mathds{1}+\vecr\cdot\vsigma)$ with $|\vecr|\le1$.
A projective two-outcome measurement is $M=\{P_\pm\}$ with
$P_\pm=\tfrac12(\mathds{1}\pm\mhat\cdot\vsigma)=\ket{m_\pm}\!\bra{m_\pm}$,
$|\mhat|=1$, and similarly $N=\{Q_\pm\}$ with axis $\nhat$ and eigenvectors
$\ket{n_\pm}$.
The outcome distributions are
\begin{equation}
  p=\Bigl(\tfrac{1+x}{2},\tfrac{1-x}{2}\Bigr),\qquad
  q=\Bigl(\tfrac{1+y}{2},\tfrac{1-y}{2}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:pq}
\end{equation}
where $x=\mhat\cdot\vecr$ and $y=\nhat\cdot\vecr$.
The overlaps are $|\langle m_\pm|n_\pm\rangle|^2=\tfrac12(1+\mhat\cdot\nhat)$ and
$|\langle m_\pm|n_\mp\rangle|^2=\tfrac12(1-\mhat\cdot\nhat)$, hence
\begin{equation}
  c=\tfrac12\bigl(1+|\mhat\cdot\nhat|\bigr)=\cos^2\tfrac{\theta}{2},
  \qquad \cos\theta=|\mhat\cdot\nhat|,
  \label{eq:c}
\end{equation}
where $\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]$ is the (acute) Bloch angle between the two
measurement axes.
Relabeling the outcomes of $N$ maps $\nhat\mapsto-\nhat$ and does not affect any
majorization statement, so we may and shall assume $\mhat\cdot\nhat=\cos\theta\ge0$.
Note that $\tfrac12\le c\le1$ and $\sqc\ge1/\sqrt2>1-\sqc$.

A general two-outcome POVM $\{E_0,E_1=\mathds{1}-E_0\}$ can always be written as
\begin{equation}
  E_0=\tfrac12\bigl[(1+\mu)\mathds{1}+\eta\,\hat a\cdot\vsigma\bigr],
  \qquad \eta\ge0,\;|\mu|+\eta\le1 ,
  \label{eq:povm}
\end{equation}
with $|\hat a|=1$, the constraint being equivalent to $0\le E_0\le\mathds{1}$.
Here $\eta$ is the sharpness and $\mu$ the bias; rank-one projective
measurements correspond to $\eta=1$, $\mu=0$.

\section{Geometry of the achievable set}
\label{sec:geometry}
% ===========================================================================

The statistics~\eqref{eq:pq} depend on $\vecr$ only through $(x,y)$.

\begin{lemma}[\cite{LiQiao2015,KTW2014,Abbott2016}]
\label{lem:ellipse}
For non-parallel axes, the set $\{(\mhat\cdot\vecr,\nhat\cdot\vecr):|\vecr|\le1\}$ is the
filled ellipse
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_\theta=\bigl\{(x,y): x^2-2xy\cos\theta+y^2\le\sin^2\theta\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Its boundary consists exactly of the images of the pure states whose Bloch
vectors lie in $\mathrm{span}\{\mhat,\nhat\}$. Moreover, for all $s,t\in\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}
  \max_{|\vecr|\le1}\,(sx+ty)=|s\mhat+t\nhat| .
  \label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\vecr=\vec r_\parallel+\vec r_\perp$ with $\vec r_\parallel\in\mathrm{span}\{\mhat,\nhat\}$.
Only $\vec r_\parallel$ contributes to $(x,y)$, and $(x,y)=G\,\xi$ where $\xi$ are the
coefficients of $\vec r_\parallel$ in the basis $\{\mhat,\nhat\}$ and
$G=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\cos\theta\\ \cos\theta&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
is the Gram matrix. Then $|\vec r_\parallel|^2=\xi^TG\xi=(x,y)G^{-1}(x,y)^T\le1$,
which is the stated inequality since $\det G=\sin^2\theta$; equality holds iff
$\vec r_\perp=0$ and $|\vecr|=1$. Conversely, every $(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}_\theta$
is attained by $\vecr=\xi_1\mhat+\xi_2\nhat$ with $\xi=G^{-1}(x,y)^T$, since then
$|\vecr|^2=(x,y)G^{-1}(x,y)^T\le1$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:support} is Cauchy--Schwarz:
$sx+ty=\vecr\cdot(s\mhat+t\nhat)$.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, $(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}_\theta$ if and only if the covariance matrix
of the observables $A=\mhat\cdot\vsigma$ and $B=\nhat\cdot\vsigma$,
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1-x^2&\cos\theta-xy\\ \cos\theta-xy&1-y^2\end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:cov}
\end{equation}
is positive semidefinite, because
$\det\Gamma=\sin^2\theta-(x^2-2xy\cos\theta+y^2)$. Its off-diagonal entry
contains the anticommutator $\frac12\{A,B\}=\cos\theta\,\mathds{1}$, which does
not depend on the state. The ellipse is a disk for mutually unbiased
measurements ($\theta=\pi/2$) and degenerates into the segment $x=y$ as
$\theta\to0$~\cite{Abbott2016}.

Consequently the achievable set
$\Ach_\oplus(M,N)=\{p(\rho)\oplus q(\rho)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^4$ is the image of
$\mathcal{E}_\theta$ under the affine map
$(x,y)\mapsto\tfrac12(1+x,1-x,1+y,1-y)$: it is convex and compact, and its
extreme points are the statistics of pure states in the plane of the two axes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ellipse}). Since $\mathcal{E}_\theta$ is centered at the origin,
moving a point radially outward to $\partial\mathcal{E}_\theta$ multiplies $|x|$
and $|y|$ by the same factor, which makes both $p$ and $q$ more ordered. Every
uncertainty measure of the form $\Phi(p)+\Psi(q)$ with $\Phi$ and $\Psi$
Schur-concave, in particular every R\'enyi and Tsallis sum, is therefore
minimized on $\partial\mathcal{E}_\theta$, and the optimization problems below
are effectively one-dimensional.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{achievable_set.pdf}
  \caption{The ellipse $\mathcal{E}_\theta$ of achievable expectation values
  $(x,y)=(\langle\mhat\cdot\vsigma\rangle,\langle\nhat\cdot\vsigma\rangle)$ for three
  Bloch angles. Markers (for $\theta=60^\circ$) show the two families of states

  (circles; $k=2$). No single point saturates all partial sums

  \label{fig:ellipse}
\end{figure}

\section{Projective measurements}
\label{sec:projective}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Direct-sum bound}

\begin{theorem}[\cite{RPZ2014,LiQiao2019}]
\label{thm:direct}
Let $M,N$ be projective qubit measurements with maximal overlap $c$. For every
state $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
  p(\rho)\oplus q(\rho)\;\maj\;\omplus:=\bigl(1,\;\sqc,\;1-\sqc,\;0\bigr),
  \label{eq:omplus}
\end{equation}
and $\omplus$ is the optimal majorization bound for $\Ach_\oplus(M,N)$.
\end{theorem}

The majorization relation~\eqref{eq:omplus} is the qubit case of the direct-sum
relation $p\oplus q\maj\{1\}\oplus W$ of Rudnicki \emph{et al.}~\cite{RPZ2014}:
for $d=2$ their vector $W=(s_1,s_2-s_1)$ has $s_1=\sqc$ and $s_2=1$. Its
optimality, and the states that saturate its partial sums, were obtained by Li
and Qiao in the majorization lattice~\cite{LiQiao2019}. We give a short proof
based on Lemma~\ref{lem:ellipse}.

\begin{proof}
We compute $S_k=\max_\rho L_k(p\oplus q)$ for $k=1,\dots,4$.
Clearly $S_1\le1$ and $S_4=2$. Since all entries are non-negative,
$S_3=2-\min_\rho\min_i(p\oplus q)_i\le2$.
Both bounds are attained by an eigenstate of $M$, for which $p=(1,0)$.
For $k=2$, the two largest entries either belong to the same distribution, with
sum $1$, or are of the form $p_i+q_j$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ellipse},
\begin{equation}
  p_i+q_j=1+\tfrac12(\pm x\pm y)\le1+\tfrac12\max\{|\mhat+\nhat|,|\mhat-\nhat|\}.
\end{equation}
With $\mhat\cdot\nhat=\cos\theta\ge0$ the maximum is
$|\mhat+\nhat|=\sqrt{2(1+\cos\theta)}=2\cos\frac\theta2=2\sqc$, so
\begin{equation}
  S_2=1+\sqc ,
  \label{eq:S2}
\end{equation}
attained by the bisector state $\vecr=(\mhat+\nhat)/|\mhat+\nhat|$. This is the qubit case
of the Landau--Pollak-type inequality $p_i+q_j\le1+\sqc$ underlying Deutsch's
relation~\cite{LandauPollak1961,Deutsch1983}. The increments of $(S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4)=(1,1+\sqc,2,2)$
give $\omplus$, which is non-increasing because $1\ge\sqc\ge1-\sqc\ge0$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sup} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tensor-product bound}

\begin{theorem}[\cite{FGG2013,PRZ2013,Partovi2011}]
\label{thm:tensor}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:direct}, for every state $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
  p(\rho)\otimes q(\rho)\;\maj\;\omtimes:=\bigl(w,\;1-w,\;0,\;0\bigr),
  \label{eq:omtimes}
\end{equation}
where $w=\frac14\bigl(1+\sqc\bigr)^2$, and $\omtimes$ is the optimal
majorization bound for $\Ach_\otimes(M,N)=\{p(\rho)\otimes q(\rho)\}$.
\end{theorem}

The vector $\omtimes$ coincides with the vector $Q^{(1)}=(C^2,1-C^2,0,0)$,
$C=(1+\sqc)/2$, of Pucha{\l}a \emph{et al.}~\cite{PRZ2013}, which for $d=2$
exhausts their hierarchy of bounds. It is also the vector of Friedland
\emph{et al.}~\cite{FGG2013}, whose entries are the increments of the maximal
partial sums $S_k$; for qubits these increments are non-increasing, so that
$\omtimes$ is the least upper bound. For mutually unbiased bases it is the
vector found by Partovi~\cite{Partovi2011}.

\begin{proof}
By the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality and Eq.~\eqref{eq:S2},
$p_iq_j\le\bigl(\tfrac{p_i+q_j}{2}\bigr)^2\le\bigl(\tfrac{1+\sqc}{2}\bigr)^2=w$,
with equality for the bisector state, for which $p_+=q_+=(1+\sqc)/2$.
Hence $S_1=w$. For an eigenstate of $M$, $p\otimes q=(q_+,q_-,0,0)$, so
$S_2=S_3=S_4=1$. Since $\sqc\ge1/\sqrt2$ we have $w\ge\frac12$, so the increments
$(w,1-w,0,0)$ are non-increasing, and Lemma~\ref{lem:sup} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Attainability}

Neither optimal bound is the statistics of a state unless the measurements
commute.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:attain}
(i) There is a state with $(p\oplus q)^\downarrow=\omplus$ if and only if $c=1$,
i.e., $M$ and $N$ commute. For $c<1$, the maxima $S_1,S_3$ are attained only by
the eigenstates of $M$ and $N$, and $S_2$ only by the bisector states
$\vecr=\pm(\mhat+\nhat)/|\mhat+\nhat|$ (for $\theta=\pi/2$ also by
$\vecr=\pm(\mhat-\nhat)/|\mhat-\nhat|$).
(ii) There is a state with $(p\otimes q)^\downarrow=\omtimes$ if and only if
$c=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $(p\oplus q)^\downarrow_1=1$ forces $\rho$ to be an eigenstate of $M$ or $N$, say
$\vecr=\pm\mhat$. Then $q=\bigl(\frac{1\pm\cos\theta}{2},\frac{1\mp\cos\theta}{2}\bigr)$ and
$(p\oplus q)^\downarrow=(1,c,1-c,0)$, which equals $\omplus$ iff $c=\sqc$, i.e.,
$c=1$. The statement about $S_2$ follows from the equality condition in
Cauchy--Schwarz and from $|\mhat-\nhat|<|\mhat+\nhat|$ for $\theta<\pi/2$.
(ii) If $c=1$, a common eigenstate of $M$ and $N$ gives
$p\otimes q=(1,0,0,0)=\omtimes$. If $c<1$, $L_2(p\otimes q)=1=L_2(\omtimes)$
requires two vanishing entries of $p\otimes q$, hence an eigenstate of $M$ or
$N$, at which $L_1(p\otimes q)=c<w=L_1(\omtimes)$, since
$w-c=\frac14(1-\sqc)(1+3\sqc)>0$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:attain} makes precise the sense in which $\omplus$ and
$\omtimes$ are \emph{descriptions} of the achievable sets rather than the
statistics of a particular state: each is a supremum in the majorization
lattice, and the partial sums of $\omplus$ are saturated by two disjoint
families of states (Fig.~\ref{fig:lorenz}). Demanding certainty about $M$
lowers the largest attainable $L_2(p\oplus q)$ from $1+\sqc$ to $1+c$; this
deficit, $\sqc(1-\sqc)$, vanishes only for commuting measurements.
Proposition~\ref{prop:attain} makes precise, for binary measurements, the
general remark of Partovi that the optimal majorization vector need not be the
statistics of any state~\cite{Partovi2011}. Once the states that saturate the
individual partial sums are known~\cite{LiQiao2019}, its proof is short; its
value lies in the consequences derived below.

\section{Entropic uncertainty relations}
\label{sec:entropic}
% ===========================================================================

Let $h(t)=-t\log_2t-(1-t)\log_2(1-t)$ be the binary entropy.

\subsection{Bounds for the R\'enyi and Tsallis families}

\begin{corollary}[\cite{RPZ2014}]
\label{cor:direct}
For every concave $\phi$ on $[0,1]$,
$\sum_i\phi(p_i)+\sum_j\phi(q_j)\ge\phi(1)+\phi(\sqc)+\phi(1-\sqc)+\phi(0)$.
In particular,
\begin{align}
  H(p)+H(q)&\;\ge\;h(\sqc) ,
  \label{eq:Hdirect}\\
  T_\gamma(p)+T_\gamma(q)&\;\ge\;\frac{1-c^{\gamma/2}-(1-\sqc)^\gamma}{\gamma-1}\qquad(\gamma>0),
  \label{eq:Tdirect}
\end{align}
and, for $0<\alpha\le1$,
\begin{equation}
  H_\alpha(p)+H_\alpha(q)\;\ge\;H_\alpha(\sqc,1-\sqc),
  \label{eq:Rdirect}
\end{equation}
where $H_\alpha(\sqc,1-\sqc)=\log_2\bigl[c^{\alpha/2}+(1-\sqc)^\alpha\bigr]/(1-\alpha)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is Karamata's inequality applied to Theorem~\ref{thm:direct}.
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Hdirect} and~\eqref{eq:Tdirect} follow with $\phi(t)=-t\log_2t$
and $\phi_\gamma(t)=(t-t^\gamma)/(\gamma-1)$, since $\sum_ip_i=\sum_jq_j=1$ gives
$T_\gamma(p)+T_\gamma(q)=\sum_k\phi_\gamma\bigl((p\oplus q)_k\bigr)$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rdirect} is the qubit case of Theorem~2 of Ref.~\cite{RPZ2014};
we recall the short argument. For $\alpha<1$ write $\sum_ip_i^\alpha=1+X$ and
$\sum_jq_j^\alpha=1+Y$ with $X,Y\ge0$. Then
\begin{align*}
  H_\alpha(p)+H_\alpha(q)&=\frac{\log_2[(1+X)(1+Y)]}{1-\alpha}\\
  &\ge\frac{\log_2(1+X+Y)}{1-\alpha},
\end{align*}
and $1+X+Y=\sum_k(p\oplus q)_k^\alpha-1\ge\sum_k(\omplus)_k^\alpha-1=c^{\alpha/2}+(1-\sqc)^\alpha$
by Schur-concavity of $v\mapsto\sum_kv_k^\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\cite{FGG2013,PRZ2013}]
\label{cor:tensor}
For every $\alpha\in[0,\infty]$,
\begin{equation}
  H_\alpha(p)+H_\alpha(q)\;\ge\;H_\alpha(\omtimes)=\frac{\log_2\!\bigl[w^\alpha+(1-w)^\alpha\bigr]}{1-\alpha}.
  \label{eq:Rtensor}
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=1$ this reads $H(p)+H(q)\ge h(w)$; for $\alpha=\infty$ it gives
$H_\infty(p)+H_\infty(q)\ge-2\log_2\frac{1+\sqc}{2}$, which is tight (bisector
state); since $H\ge H_\infty$, it contains Deutsch's Shannon
bound~\cite{Deutsch1983}, which has the same right-hand side.
\end{corollary}

As noted in Sec.~\ref{sec:prelim}, the two forms suit different families of
entropies. Where both apply, the direct sum is stronger, but it cannot be
extended to all orders. That the direct-sum R\'enyi relation does not hold for
$\alpha>1$ was stated in Ref.~\cite{RPZ2014} (Sec.~III); part~(ii) below gives
an explicit counterexample:

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:compare}
(i) For $0<\alpha\le1$, $H_\alpha(\sqc,1-\sqc)\ge H_\alpha(\omtimes)$, with
equality iff $c=1$; in particular $h(\sqc)\ge h(w)$.
(ii) For $c<1$ the direct-sum form~\eqref{eq:Rdirect} fails at $\alpha=\infty$:
$\min_\rho[H_\infty(p)+H_\infty(q)]=-2\log_2\frac{1+\sqc}{2}<-\frac12\log_2c=H_\infty(\sqc,1-\sqc)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Since $w-\sqc=\frac14(1-\sqc)^2\ge0$, we have $(\sqc,1-\sqc)\maj(w,1-w)$,
with equality iff $c=1$; apply strict Schur-concavity. The analogous statement
in any dimension is proven in Ref.~\cite{RPZ2014}.
(ii) With $t=\sqc$, $\bigl(\frac{1+t}{2}\bigr)^2-t=\bigl(\frac{1-t}{2}\bigr)^2>0$
for $t<1$; the minimum is the tight bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:tensor}.
\end{proof}

The failure, however, sets in only beyond $\alpha=2$. Numerically, for qubits
$H_\alpha(\sqc,1-\sqc)$ is a valid bound for every $\alpha\le2$ at every angle,
and it fails above an angle-dependent order between $2.00$ ($\theta\to0$) and
$2.58$ ($\theta\to90^\circ$). At $\alpha=2$ the bisector state is optimal at
every angle~\cite{ZozorBosykPortesi2013}, and with $s=\sqc$ and
$a=\frac{1+s}{2}$ the exact minimum is
$-2\log_2[a^2+(1-a)^2]=-2\log_2\frac{1+s^2}{2}$, whereas
$H_2(s,1-s)=-\log_2[s^2+(1-s)^2]$; the bound holds iff
$\bigl(\frac{1+s^2}{2}\bigr)^2\le s^2+(1-s)^2$, i.e., iff
$\frac14(s-1)^3(s+3)\le0$, which is true for $s\le1$. The direct-sum form thus
extends well beyond the range $\alpha\le1$ in which it is proven in general.

\subsection{When are the bounds tight?}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:tight}
Let $c<1$.
(i) For every continuous, strictly Schur-concave $\Phi$,
\begin{equation}
  \min_\rho\Phi(p\oplus q)>\Phi(\omplus),\qquad
  \min_\rho\Phi(p\otimes q)>\Phi(\omtimes).
\end{equation}
Consequently the bounds~\eqref{eq:Hdirect}, \eqref{eq:Tdirect}
and~\eqref{eq:Rdirect}, and the bounds~\eqref{eq:Rtensor} with
$0<\alpha<\infty$, are all strict.
(ii) The bounds~\eqref{eq:Rtensor} of order $\alpha=0$ and $\alpha=\infty$ are
tight; they are attained by the eigenstates of $M$ or $N$ and by the bisector
states, respectively.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The achievable statistics form a compact set, so the minimum of $\Phi$ is
attained at some state $\rho^\ast$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:direct} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:attain}(i), $p\oplus q(\rho^\ast)\maj\omplus$ with
$(p\oplus q)^\downarrow\neq\omplus$, hence $\Phi(p\oplus q(\rho^\ast))>\Phi(\omplus)$.
The tensor product is analogous, by Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:attain}(ii). The Shannon and Tsallis sums are strictly
Schur-concave functions of $p\oplus q$, and $H_\alpha(p)+H_\alpha(q)=H_\alpha(p\otimes q)$
with $H_\alpha$ strictly Schur-concave for $0<\alpha<\infty$. For~\eqref{eq:Rdirect}
with $\alpha<1$, apply the argument to $v\mapsto\sum_kv_k^\alpha$ in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:direct}.
(ii) An eigenstate of $M$ gives $H_0(p)+H_0(q)=0+1=H_0(\omtimes)$, and a bisector
state gives $\max_ip_i\max_jq_j=w$.
\end{proof}

The proof of (i) uses only that the achievable statistics form a compact set
whose least upper bound is not attained. It therefore applies whenever the
optimal bound is not attained: to every pair of qubit POVMs whose effects do not
commute (Proposition~\ref{prop:povm-attain}) and to both bounds for binary
projective measurements in any dimension with $c<1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dimd}).

Theorem~\ref{thm:tight} makes precise in what sense the majorization bounds are
optimal. As majorization bounds they cannot be improved
(Theorems~\ref{thm:direct} and~\ref{thm:tensor}); yet, because the supremum is
not the statistics of any state, the bound it induces is strict for every
strictly Schur-concave measure. For such measures the optimum has to be found
measure by measure, which is what entropy-specific relations do; only at the
extreme orders $\alpha=0$ and $\alpha=\infty$ does the majorization bound reach
it.

\subsection{Comparison with the Maassen--Uffink relation and with the exact optimum}

Figure~\ref{fig:entropic} compares the Shannon bounds with MU and with the
exact minimum of $H(p)+H(q)$, which is known for
qubits~\cite{SanchezRuiz1998,Ghirardi2003}. By Sec.~\ref{sec:geometry} the
minimum lies on the great circle through $\mhat$ and $\nhat$. For
$\theta\le\bar\theta$ it is attained at the bisector states and equals
$2h\bigl(\frac{1+\sqc}{2}\bigr)$~\cite{Ghirardi2003}. At $\bar\theta$ the
bisector turns from a local minimum into a local maximum along the circle; a
second-derivative test shows that this happens when
$\sqrt{\bar c}\,\artanh\sqrt{\bar c}=1$~\cite{Bosyk2011}, i.e., at $\bar c\approx0.6948$ and
$\bar\theta\approx67.07^\circ$. For $\bar\theta<\theta<\pi/2$ the minimizers
bifurcate and the minimum has to be computed numerically~\cite{Ghirardi2003}.
As $\theta$ grows they move continuously towards the eigenstates of $M$ and
$N$, which they reach at $\theta=\pi/2$, where MU is tight.

The range in which the bisector is optimal depends on the uncertainty measure,
as Zozor \emph{et al.} showed for R\'enyi
entropies~\cite{ZozorBosykPortesi2013}; that the relative uncertainty of qubit
states depends on the measure reflects a lack of majorization between their
statistics~\cite{LuisBosykPortesi2016}.
For $c>\frac12$ the eigenstates are optimal for $\alpha\le\alpha_0(c)$, the
bisector for $\alpha\ge\alpha^\star(c)$, and intermediate states in between.
Here $\alpha_0$ exceeds $\frac12$ (about $0.52$, $0.56$ and $0.66$ at $20^\circ$,
$60^\circ$ and $85^\circ$), and $\alpha^\star$ decreases from $\alpha^\dagger\approx1.43$ as
$c\to\frac12$ to $\frac12$ as $c\to1$; for mutually unbiased measurements the
eigenstates remain optimal for all $\alpha<\alpha^\dagger$, and the bisector
takes over at $\alpha^\dagger$. In terms of the Bloch angle, the bisector
is optimal up to $43^\circ$ for $\alpha=3/4$, up to $\bar\theta\approx67^\circ$
for the Shannon entropy and up to $80^\circ$ for $\alpha=1.2$, and at all angles
for $\alpha\ge\alpha^\dagger$, in line with the tightness of the min-entropy
bound. Here $\alpha^\dagger$ solves
$2H_\alpha\bigl(\frac{2+\sqrt2}{4},\frac{2-\sqrt2}{4}\bigr)=1$, the condition for
bisector and eigenstates to perform equally well when the measurements are
mutually unbiased. For the max-entropy, for instance, the eigenstates give
$\log_2(1+\sin\theta)$ and the bisector $2\log_2(1+\sin\frac\theta2)$.

For mutually unbiased measurements ($\theta=90^\circ$, $c=\frac12$) MU is tight
(1 bit), while $h(1/\sqrt2)\approx0.872$ and $h(w)\approx0.844$. As the
measurements become more compatible, however, the majorization bounds take
over: $h(\sqc)>-\log_2c$ for $\sqc>t^\ast\approx0.7585$, i.e., for Bloch angles
$\theta<\theta^\ast\approx81.3^\circ$ (the tensor bound overtakes MU at $\theta<79.3^\circ$), as
reported numerically in Ref.~\cite{Baek2019}; earlier majorization
bounds~\cite{FGG2013,PRZ2013} were compared with MU and with the optimal qubit
bound in Ref.~\cite{KTW2014}.
For the Shannon entropy alone, however, neither majorization bound is the best
closed-form relation available. The bound
\begin{equation}
  H(p)+H(q)\;\ge\;h\Bigl(\frac{1+\sqrt{2c-1}}{2}\Bigr)
  \label{eq:SR}
\end{equation}
of Refs.~\cite{SanchezRuiz1998,KTW2014} (see also Ref.~\cite{Coles2017}), where
$2c-1=\cos\theta$, is strictly stronger than $h(\sqc)$ at every angle and at least
as strong as MU, with which it coincides, and hence is tight, at $\theta=90^\circ$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:entropic}); at $\theta=60^\circ$, for instance, it gives
$0.601$~bit against $0.568$ for $h(\sqc)$. What majorization offers instead is a
single bound for all Schur-concave measures at once.

As $c\to1$ the advantage over MU becomes unbounded, and the direct-sum bound
becomes tight in ratio. With $\varepsilon=1-\sqc\approx\theta^2/8$ one has
$-\log_2c\approx2\varepsilon/\ln2$ and
$h(\sqc)=h(\varepsilon)\approx\varepsilon\log_2(e/\varepsilon)$, whereas the
exact minimum is $2h(\varepsilon/2)\approx\varepsilon\log_2(2e/\varepsilon)$.
To leading order the ratio of the exact minimum to $h(\sqc)$ is therefore
$1+1/\log_2(e/\varepsilon)$: it tends to $1$, but only logarithmically ($1.09$
at $\theta=0.1$~rad, $1.047$ at $\theta=0.003$~rad), while the ratio of $h(\sqc)$ to MU
grows as $\frac12\ln(8e/\theta^2)$ ($3.8$ and $7.3$). The eigenstates of $M$, although
they saturate $S_1$ and $S_3$, give $h(c)$, asymptotically twice the minimum
(the ratio approaches $2$ slowly; it is $1.67$ and $1.82$ at the same angles).

Between these limits the strictness of Theorem~\ref{thm:tight} is far from
marginal. Over all angles the direct-sum bound $h(\sqc)$ reaches at least
$78\%$ of the exact minimum, the smallest ratio, $0.782$, occurring at
$\theta\approx70^\circ$, just beyond $\bar\theta$, where the minimizer leaves the
bisector; the absolute gap is largest, $0.196$~bit, at $\theta\approx74^\circ$,
and the tensor-product bound $h(w)$ reaches at least $76.1\%$ of the exact
minimum (at $\theta\approx70.6^\circ$) and falls short by up to $0.217$~bit. At
$\theta=90^\circ$, where MU is tight, $h(\sqc)$ still misses the exact value
$1$~bit by $0.128$~bit. Wherever a single strictly Schur-concave measure is
what matters, such as the Shannon entropy in the analysis of key distribution,
this gap is the price of universality, and the entropy-specific optimum should
be used.

Finally, MU is one member of a family $H_\alpha(p)+H_\beta(q)\ge-\log_2c$ with
$1/\alpha+1/\beta=2$~\cite{MaassenUffink1988}, which includes the pair
$H_\infty(p)+H_{1/2}(q)\ge-\log_2c$ of min- and max-entropy. Majorization
relations of the form~\eqref{eq:maj-UR} are symmetric under the exchange of $p$
and $q$ and therefore yield such mixed-order relations only indirectly, through
the monotonicity of $H_\alpha$ in $\alpha$. Conditional majorization bounds,
which constrain $q$ for a given $p$, yield them directly: for qubits they give
the full uncertainty region of any pair $(H_\alpha,H_\beta)$~\cite{XiaoFangGour2019}.

\section{General two-outcome qubit POVMs}
\label{sec:povm}
% ===========================================================================

For POVMs, the partial sums of the direct-sum relation are largest eigenvalues
of sums of effects, $S_k=\max_{|J|=k}\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\sum_{j\in J}K_j\bigr)$,
where the $K_j$ run over the effects of both
measurements~\cite{Baek2019} (see also
Refs.~\cite{RPZ2014,RasteginZyczkowski2016,Bosyk2014}); Baek \emph{et al.}
evaluated them for unbiased unsharp qubit observables~\cite{Baek2019}, and
the flattened vector these partial sums define is optimal for general
measurements~\cite{Yuan2023}. For two-outcome qubit POVMs, whose achievable
expectation values were described geometrically in Ref.~\cite{Abbott2016}, the
maximization can be carried out in closed form for arbitrary bias, and the
resulting vector turns out to be ordered, so that no flattening is needed.

Let $M=\{E_0,E_1\}$ with parameters $(\mu,\eta,\hat a)$ and $N=\{F_0,F_1\}$ with
$(\nu,\zeta,\hat b)$, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:povm}. Then
$p=\frac12\bigl(1+\mu+\eta\,\hat a\cdot\vecr,\;1-\mu-\eta\,\hat a\cdot\vecr\bigr)$, and
similarly for $q$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:povm}
Define
\begin{align}
  \kappa&=\max\{|\mu|+\eta,\;|\nu|+\zeta\},\\
  \Lambda&=\max_{s,t=\pm1}\bigl[s\mu+t\nu+|s\eta\,\hat a+t\zeta\,\hat b|\bigr].
\end{align}
Then $p(\rho)\oplus q(\rho)\maj\omplus(M,N)$ for all $\rho$, where
\begin{equation}
  \omplus(M,N)=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\kappa,\;1+\Lambda-\kappa,\;1+\kappa-\Lambda,\;1-\kappa\bigr)
  \label{eq:om-povm}
\end{equation}
is the optimal majorization bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The largest single outcome probability is
$\max_\rho\frac12\bigl(1+|\mu+\eta\,\hat a\cdot\vecr|\bigr)=\frac12(1+|\mu|+\eta)$ for $M$,
so $S_1=\frac12(1+\kappa)$; likewise the smallest is $\frac12(1-\kappa)$, so
$S_3=2-\frac12(1-\kappa)$. For $S_2$, a pair within one distribution sums to $1$,
while a cross pair gives, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:support},
\begin{multline}
  \max_\rho\bigl[1+\tfrac12\bigl(s(\mu+\eta\,\hat a\cdot\vecr)+t(\nu+\zeta\,\hat b\cdot\vecr)\bigr)\bigr]\\
  =1+\tfrac12\bigl[s\mu+t\nu+|s\eta\hat a+t\zeta\hat b|\bigr].
\end{multline}
Choosing $s=\operatorname{sgn}\mu$ and $t=\operatorname{sgn}\nu$ (either sign if
zero) shows $\Lambda\ge0$,
so $S_2=1+\frac12\Lambda$. The increments of $(S_k)$ are the entries of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:om-povm}. They are non-increasing iff $\kappa\le\Lambda\le2\kappa$.
The upper inequality follows from the triangle inequality,
$\Lambda\le|\mu|+|\nu|+\eta+\zeta\le2\kappa$. For the lower one, let $\rho^\ast$
attain $S_1$ via an outcome of, say, $M$; then
$S_2\ge S_1+\max_jq_j(\rho^\ast)\ge S_1+\frac12$, i.e., $\Lambda\ge\kappa$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sup} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

For projective measurements $\kappa=1$, $\Lambda=2\sqc$ and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:om-povm} reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:omplus}. For unbiased
measurements ($\mu=\nu=0$),
$\Lambda=(\eta^2+\zeta^2+2\eta\zeta|\hat a\cdot\hat b|)^{1/2}$, in agreement with
Ref.~\cite{Baek2019}. If moreover $\eta=\zeta$, then
$\omplus(M,N)=\eta\,\omplus+\frac{1-\eta}{2}(1,1,1,1)$, where $\omplus$ is the
vector~\eqref{eq:omplus} of the sharp measurements along $\hat a$ and $\hat b$:
such measurements are noisy versions of sharp ones~\cite{Busch1986,Baek2019}, and
the achievable statistics, their supremum and the saturating states are those of
the sharp case mixed with the uniform vector. For unequal sharpness or nonzero
bias, $\omplus(M,N)$ is not of this form, and the relevant geometric quantities
are the lengths $|\eta\hat a\pm\zeta\hat b|$ rather than the overlap $c$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:povm} also covers states of fixed spectrum: projective
measurements on states with $|\vecr|=r$ have the statistics of unbiased
measurements of sharpness $\eta=\zeta=r$ on pure states, so the
spectrum-dependent bound~\cite{LiQiao2019,Puchala2018} is
$\frac12\bigl(1+r,\,1-r+2r\sqc,\,1+r-2r\sqc,\,1-r\bigr)$, and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:povm-attain} below it is not attained for $r>0$ and
$c<1$.

As for projective measurements, the bound is not attained when the effects do
not commute; with bias, it may not be attained even when they do.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:povm-attain}
Let $M$ and $N$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:povm}.
(i) If $E_0$ and $F_0$ do not commute, i.e., $\eta\zeta\,\hat a\times\hat b\neq0$,
no state attains $\omplus(M,N)$.
(ii) If $\mu=\nu=0$ and $E_0$ and $F_0$ commute, a state attains $\omplus(M,N)$.
(iii) With bias, commuting effects need not attain it: for $\hat b=\hat a$,
$(\mu,\eta)=(0.3,0.6)$ and $(\nu,\zeta)=(-0.8,0.15)$, $S_1$ is attained only at
$\vecr=-\hat a$ and $S_2$ only at $\vecr=\hat a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Suppose a state attains $S_1=\frac12(1+\kappa)$ through an outcome of $M$,
so that $\kappa=|\mu|+\eta$; the case of $N$ is symmetric. Since $\eta>0$, this
forces $\vecr=s_0\hat a$ with $s_0\mu=|\mu|$. There the two largest entries of
$p\oplus q$ sum to
$1+\frac12\max_t\bigl[|\mu|+\eta+t\nu+ts_0\zeta\,\hat a\cdot\hat b\bigr]$,
whereas the term $s=s_0$ of $\Lambda$ gives
$S_2\ge1+\frac12\bigl[|\mu|+t\nu+|\eta\hat a+ts_0\zeta\hat b|\bigr]$ for each
$t$. By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$|\eta\hat a+ts_0\zeta\hat b|>\eta+ts_0\zeta\,\hat a\cdot\hat b$ unless
$\hat b=\pm\hat a$ (and $\eta\pm ts_0\zeta\ge0$), which is excluded when $E_0$ and
$F_0$ do not commute, so no state attains both $S_1$ and $S_2$.
(ii) If one measurement is trivial, say $\eta=0$, then $\Lambda=\kappa=\zeta$,
and $\vecr=\hat b$ gives $(p\oplus q)^\downarrow=\frac12(1+\zeta,1,1,1-\zeta)
=\omplus(M,N)$. Otherwise, up to relabeling the outcomes of $N$, $\hat b=\hat a$,
so that $\Lambda=\eta+\zeta$ and $\kappa=\max\{\eta,\zeta\}$, and $\vecr=\hat a$
gives $(p\oplus q)^\downarrow=\frac12(1+\kappa,1+\Lambda-\kappa,1+\kappa-\Lambda,
1-\kappa)$.
(iii) Here $\kappa=0.95$ is reached only by the second outcome of $N$ at
$\vecr=-\hat a$, and $\Lambda=1.55$ only by $(s,t)=(1,-1)$ at $\vecr=\hat a$.
\end{proof}

Bias and unequal sharpness arise naturally in photon counting. If the two
outputs of a polarizing beam splitter are monitored by detectors of
efficiencies $\eta_H$ and $\eta_V$, and no-click events are assigned to either
outcome at random, the effect of outcome $H$ has the form~\eqref{eq:povm} with
$\hat a=\hat z$, $\mu=(\eta_H-\eta_V)/2$ and $\eta=(\eta_H+\eta_V)/2$. A
mismatch of detection efficiencies, a known concern in quantum key
distribution~\cite{Makarov2006}, thus appears as a bias. A single
click/no-click detector behind a polarizer, with no-click events counted as the
second outcome, gives $\mu=\eta_d-1$ and $\eta=\eta_d$, so that $|\mu|+\eta=1$.

\emph{Uncertainty versus incompatibility.}---For unbiased POVMs the vectors
$\eta\hat a\pm\zeta\hat b$ that determine $\Lambda$ also decide joint
measurability: the pair is jointly measurable if and only if
$|\eta\hat a+\zeta\hat b|+|\eta\hat a-\zeta\hat b|\le2$~\cite{Busch1986,HMZ2016};
for biased POVMs the criterion is more involved and no longer depends on these
vectors alone~\cite{StanoReitznerHeinosaari2008,BuschSchmidt2010,Yu2010}.
The two notions are nevertheless distinct. For orthogonal axes and
$\eta=\zeta\le1/\sqrt2$ the measurements are jointly measurable, yet
$S_2=1+\eta/\sqrt2$ stays below the value $1+\eta$ that would be reached if both
outcome distributions could be sharpened at once, and $\omplus(M,N)$ is not
attained (Proposition~\ref{prop:povm-attain}). Attainment requires commuting
effects, whether or not the pair is jointly measurable, and with bias even
commuting effects need not attain the bound: in the example of
Proposition~\ref{prop:povm-attain}(iii) the two readouts of the same axis favor
opposite ends of it, a trade-off of classical origin. Majorization uncertainty
relations therefore quantify preparation uncertainty, which persists in the
absence of measurement incompatibility.

As a corollary, $H(p)+H(q)\ge-\sum_k\omega_k\log_2\omega_k$ with
$\omega=\omplus(M,N)$ for all two-outcome qubit POVMs; by
Proposition~\ref{prop:povm-attain} and Theorem~\ref{thm:tight} this bound is
strict whenever $E_0$ and $F_0$ do not commute. Figure~\ref{fig:povm} shows that
it tracks the exact minimum as a function of sharpness, becoming exact in the
trivial limit $\eta\to0$, and of bias, where the gap grows with the bias at
$45^\circ$ but not at $90^\circ$.

\emph{Imperfect detectors and uncertainty-based tests.}---Experiments that
test uncertainty relations, or use them to certify entanglement or steering,
usually compare their data with the bound for ideal measurements. With the
detector models above, Theorem~\ref{thm:povm} tells exactly when this is
justified.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:detectors}
Let $M,N$ be two-outcome qubit POVMs with axes $\hat a,\hat b$, and let
$M^\circ,N^\circ$ be the projective measurements along the same axes, with
statistics $p^\circ,q^\circ$ and overlap $c=\frac12(1+|\hat a\cdot\hat b|)<1$.
(i) If $\mu=\nu=0$, then $p(\rho)\oplus q(\rho)\maj p^\circ(\rho)\oplus q^\circ(\rho)$
for every $\rho$; hence every lower bound on $\Phi(p\oplus q)$ or on
$\Phi(p)+\Psi(q)$, with $\Phi,\Psi$ Schur-concave, that holds for
$M^\circ,N^\circ$ also holds for $M,N$.
(ii) In general, the ideal relation $p(\rho)\oplus q(\rho)\maj\omplus$ holds for
all $\rho$ if and only if $\Lambda\le2\sqc$. If $\Lambda>2\sqc$, some state violates
the Landau--Pollak relation $p_i+q_j\le1+\sqc$ of the ideal measurements.
(iii) If $M$ and $N$ are read out by the same detectors, $\mu=\nu$ and
$\eta=\zeta$, with $\hat a\cdot\hat b\ge0$, the condition reads
$|\mu|\le\sqc\,(1-\eta)$. Click/no-click detection violates it for every
$\eta_d<1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) For $\mu=0$, $p=\eta p^\circ+(1-\eta)u$ with $u=(\frac12,\frac12)$, i.e.,
$p=Dp^\circ$ for the doubly stochastic matrix
$D=\frac12\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1+\eta&1-\eta\\1-\eta&1+\eta\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$;
likewise $q=D'q^\circ$. Hence $p\maj p^\circ$, $q\maj q^\circ$ and, since $D\oplus D'$
is doubly stochastic, $p\oplus q\maj p^\circ\oplus q^\circ$~\cite{MarshallOlkin2011};
Schur-concavity gives the claim. (ii) By Theorems~\ref{thm:direct}
and~\ref{thm:povm}, $\omplus$ and $\omplus(M,N)$ are lattice suprema. If
$\omplus(M,N)\maj\omplus$, transitivity gives $p\oplus q\maj\omplus$ for all states;
conversely, if $\omplus$ bounds all $p\oplus q$, then $\omplus(M,N)=\sup\maj\omplus$.
Since $S_1=\frac12(1+\kappa)\le1$ and $S_3=\frac12(3+\kappa)\le2$ always, the
relation holds iff $S_2=1+\frac12\Lambda\le1+\sqc$. If $\Lambda>2\sqc$, the state that
attains $S_2$ gives $p_i+q_j=1+\frac12\Lambda>1+\sqc$ for some $i,j$. (iii) For
$\mu=\nu$, $\eta=\zeta$ and $\hat a\cdot\hat b\ge0$, the terms of $\Lambda$ are
$2|\mu|+\eta|\hat a+\hat b|=2|\mu|+2\eta\sqc$ for $s=t$ and
$\eta|\hat a-\hat b|\le2\eta\sqc$ for $s=-t$, so $\Lambda=2|\mu|+2\eta\sqc$. For
click/no-click detection $|\mu|=1-\eta_d$, and the condition becomes
$(1-\eta_d)(1-\sqc)\le0$, which fails for $\eta_d<1$ and $c<1$.
\end{proof}

Noise only blurs the statistics towards the uniform distribution, but bias
shifts the image of $\mathcal{E}_\theta$, and the shifted ellipse can leave the
ideal one. Whether a particular entropic bound is violated depends on the
measure: the Shannon minimum is more forgiving than the Landau--Pollak sum, but
not immune (Fig.~\ref{fig:detectors}). For orthogonal axes and random no-click
assignment, an efficiency $\eta_H=0.99$ violates the ideal relation for
$\eta_V<0.942$ and the ideal Shannon bound of one bit for $\eta_V<0.578$; for
$\eta_H=0.95$ the thresholds are $0.708$ and $0.211$, and for $\eta_H=0.9$ the
Shannon bound is never violated, although the ideal relation still is for
$\eta_V<0.417$. Click/no-click detectors lower the Shannon minimum below its
ideal value at all efficiencies and angles we examined; for orthogonal axes this
holds for every $\eta_d<1$, since the eigenstate of the dark outcome of $M$ gives
$H(M)+H(N)=h(\eta_d/2)<1$. In all cases $H(\omplus(M,N))$ remains a valid bound.

This matters for steering tests~\cite{Walborn2011,Schneeloch2013,Uola2020}, in
which Bob trusts his measurements and Alice's are uncharacterized.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:steering}
If Alice's measurements $A_1,A_2$ and Bob's two-outcome measurements $M,N$
admit a local-hidden-state model, then
\begin{multline}
  H(M|A_1)+H(N|A_2)\;\ge\;\min_\rho\bigl[H(M)+H(N)\bigr]\\
  \ge\;H\bigl(\omplus(M,N)\bigr).
  \label{eq:steering}
\end{multline}
The minimum for the ideal measurements $M^\circ,N^\circ$ may replace the
right-hand side if $M,N$ are unbiased, but not in general: for click/no-click
detection along orthogonal axes, a product state with Bob in the dark eigenstate
of $M$ gives $H(M|A_1)+H(N|A_2)=h(\eta_d/2)$, below the ideal value of one bit
for every $\eta_d<1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In a local-hidden-state model~\cite{Uola2020},
$p(a,b|x,y)=\sum_\lambda p(\lambda)\,p(a|x,\lambda)\,\tr(\rho_\lambda K^y_b)$, where $K^y_b$ are
the effects of Bob's measurement $y\in\{M,N\}$. Conditioning on $\lambda$ cannot
increase entropy, and given $\lambda$ Bob's outcome is independent of Alice's, so
$H(B_y|A_x)\ge H(B_y|A_x\Lambda)=H(B_y|\Lambda)=\sum_\lambda p(\lambda)H(B_y)_{\rho_\lambda}$.
Summing over $(x,y)=(1,M),(2,N)$ gives the first inequality in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:steering}~\cite{Walborn2011,Schneeloch2013}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:povm} the second. For the example, $\hat a=\hat z$,
$\hat b=\hat x$, $E_0=\eta_d\ket{0}\!\bra{0}$ and Bob in $\ket{1}$: $H(M)=0$ and
$H(N)=h(\eta_d/2)$, independent of Alice's outcomes. For unbiased measurements,
Proposition~\ref{prop:detectors}(i) shows that the ideal minimum is a valid
right-hand side.
\end{proof}

An uncertainty-based steering or entanglement test with biased trusted
detectors must therefore use the bound of the measurements actually performed.
Theorem~\ref{thm:povm} provides it in closed form, for every Schur-concave
measure at once, and Proposition~\ref{prop:detectors} tells when the ideal bound
can be kept.

\section{Binary projective measurements in dimension \texorpdfstring{$d$}{d}}
\label{sec:dimd}
% ===========================================================================

The qubit analysis already contains the general binary projective case.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:dimd}
Let $M=\{P,\mathds{1}-P\}$ and $N=\{Q,\mathds{1}-Q\}$ be projective measurements on
$\mathbb{C}^d$, write $P_1=P$, $P_2=\mathds{1}-P$, $Q_1=Q$, $Q_2=\mathds{1}-Q$, and
let $c=\max_{i,j}\|P_iQ_j\|^2$, where $\|\cdot\|$ is the operator norm. Then
Theorems~\ref{thm:direct} and~\ref{thm:tensor} hold verbatim, and $\omplus$ and
$\omtimes$ are attained if and only if $c=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the eigenvalue formula of Sec.~\ref{sec:povm}, $S_k$ for the direct sum is
the largest eigenvalue of a sum of $k$ of the four projectors. Hence $S_1=1$,
$S_3=2$, since any three of them include $P_1+P_2=\mathds{1}$ or
$Q_1+Q_2=\mathds{1}$, and $S_2=\max_{i,j}\lambda_{\max}(P_i+Q_j)=1+\sqc$, since
$\lambda_{\max}(P+Q)=1+\|PQ\|$ for any two projectors~\cite{Halmos1969}.
Moreover $c\ge\frac12$: if $Q_1\neq0$ and $\psi$ is a unit vector in its range,
then $\|P_1\psi\|^2+\|P_2\psi\|^2=1$, so $\max_i\|P_iQ_1\|^2\ge\frac12$ (and if
$Q_1=0$, then $Q_2=\mathds{1}$ and $c=1$). The increments therefore give
$\omplus$, which is ordered because $1\ge\sqc\ge1-\sqc$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:sup} yields Theorem~\ref{thm:direct}. For the tensor product,
$p_iq_j\le\bigl(\frac{p_i+q_j}{2}\bigr)^2\le w$, with equality at a top
eigenvector of $P_i+Q_j$ with $\|P_iQ_j\|^2=c$. Indeed, by Jordan's
lemma~\cite{Halmos1969}, which is also the standard tool for entropic
uncertainty relations of binary measurements~\cite{KTW2014}, $\mathbb{C}^d$
decomposes into mutually orthogonal subspaces of dimension one or two that are
invariant under $P$ and $Q$; on a two-dimensional subspace with overlap $c$, the
bisector state of the qubit measurements that $P$ and $Q$ induce there gives
$p_i=q_j=(1+\sqc)/2$, and for $c=1$ a common eigenvector gives $p_i=q_j=1$.
Hence $S_1=w$, while a state in the range of $P$ gives $S_2=S_3=S_4=1$; since
$c\ge\frac12$ implies $w\ge\frac12$, the increments $(w,1-w,0,0)$ are ordered,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor} follows.

If $c<1$, a state with $L_1(p\oplus q)=1$ lies in the range of some $P_i$ (or
$Q_j$); then $q_j=\tr(\rho P_iQ_jP_i)\le\|P_iQ_j\|^2\le c$ for all $j$, so
$L_2(p\oplus q)\le1+c<1+\sqc$ and $\omplus$ is not attained. Likewise,
$L_2(p\otimes q)=1$ requires two vanishing entries of $p\otimes q$, hence such
a state, at which $L_1(p\otimes q)\le c<w$, so $\omtimes$ is not attained
either. If $c=1$, the ranges of some $P_i$ and $Q_j$ intersect, and a common
eigenvector attains $\omplus=(1,1,0,0)$ and $\omtimes=(1,0,0,0)$.
\end{proof}

In particular, all bounds of Sec.~\ref{sec:entropic} hold for binary projective
measurements in any dimension with $c=\max_{i,j}\|P_iQ_j\|^2$, the min-entropy
bound remains tight, and for $c<1$ the bounds for strictly Schur-concave
measures are strict (Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}).
The direct-sum part of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimd} follows from the eigenvalue
formula~\cite{Baek2019} and $\lambda_{\max}(P+Q)=1+\|PQ\|$~\cite{Halmos1969}, and
the tensor part from the reduction to qubit blocks by Jordan's
lemma~\cite{Halmos1969,KTW2014}; new are the tensor bound and the attainment
conditions in dimension $d$.
Equivalently, $c=\frac12+\frac14\|\{A,B\}\|$ with $A=2P-\mathds{1}$ and
$B=2Q-\mathds{1}$, as shown in any dimension by Kaniewski
\emph{et al.}~\cite{KTW2014}. Indeed, on a two-dimensional block, where $P$ and $Q$ act as
qubit projectors with axes $\mhat_b$ and $\nhat_b$,
$\{A,B\}=2(\mhat_b\cdot\nhat_b)\mathds{1}$ and
$c_b=\frac12(1+|\mhat_b\cdot\nhat_b|)$, while $\{A,B\}=\pm2$ on a
one-dimensional block. This relation between the overlap and the effective
anticommutator is closely related to the anticommutator norm formula
$\|PQ+QP\|=\|PQ\|+\|PQ\|^2$~\cite{Walters2016}.

The argument for the direct sum uses only the eigenvalue formula, so it carries
over to binary POVMs in any dimension.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:binary-povm}
Let $M=\{E_1,E_2\}$ and $N=\{F_1,F_2\}$ be two-outcome POVMs on $\mathbb{C}^d$, let
$S_1$ be the largest eigenvalue of the four effects, and let
$S_2=\max_{i,j}\lambda_{\max}(E_i+F_j)$. Then
\begin{equation}
  p(\rho)\oplus q(\rho)\;\maj\;\bigl(S_1,\;S_2-S_1,\;1+S_1-S_2,\;1-S_1\bigr)
  \qquad\forall\rho,
  \label{eq:om-binary}
\end{equation}
and this vector is the optimal majorization bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the eigenvalue formula, $S_1$ and $S_2$ are the maximal partial sums (a pair
within one measurement sums to $1$, which never exceeds the cross terms, since
$S_2\ge S_1+\frac12$ as shown below), and $S_3=1+S_1$, since any three effects include $E_1+E_2=\mathds{1}$ or
$F_1+F_2=\mathds{1}$. The increments are non-increasing iff
$S_1+\frac12\le S_2\le2S_1$. The upper inequality holds because $\lambda_{\max}$ is
subadditive and $S_1\ge\frac12$. For the lower one, a top eigenvector of the
effect that gives $S_1$ is a state at which the other measurement has an
outcome of probability at least $\frac12$. Lemma~\ref{lem:sup} completes the
proof.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:povm} is the qubit case, with $S_1=\frac12(1+\kappa)$ and
$S_2=1+\frac12\Lambda$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:dimd} the projective case, with
$S_1=1$ and $S_2=1+\sqc$. Proposition~\ref{prop:detectors}(ii) generalizes in
the same way: since $S_1\le1$ and $S_3=1+S_1\le2$ always, two binary POVMs obey
the relation $p\oplus q\maj(1,\sqc,1-\sqc,0)$ of a reference pair of projective
measurements with overlap $c$ iff $S_2\le1+\sqc$. For arbitrary POVMs in any
dimension, the eigenvalue
formula together with Lemma~\ref{lem:sup} gives an exact numerical procedure,
which we use to verify Theorems~\ref{thm:povm} and~\ref{thm:dimd} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:binary-povm} independently (Appendix~\ref{app:numerics}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ===========================================================================

For binary measurements the majorization approach to uncertainty can be
carried out in closed form. For a qubit the achievable statistics form an
affine image of an ellipse, and a single four-component vector, determined by
the overlap $c$, is their least upper bound in the majorization lattice, for the
direct sum as well as for the tensor product. For POVMs the direct-sum bound is
determined by $\kappa$ and $\Lambda$, and in any dimension by the largest
eigenvalues of the effects and of their pairwise sums
(Corollary~\ref{cor:binary-povm}). This is the unified perspective that
majorization offers: one vector controls every Schur-concave uncertainty
measure at once.

The comparison with entropy-specific relations shows what this unification
costs. Because the supremum is not the statistics of any state when the
measurements do not commute, the induced bound is strict for every strictly
Schur-concave measure, and it becomes exact only at the extreme orders
$\alpha=0$ and $\alpha=\infty$. Against a generic scalar relation such as MU,
on the other hand, the majorization bounds are strong. For two projective qubit
measurements, both Shannon bounds depend only on the Bloch angle $\theta$
between the two measurement axes: the direct-sum bound is $h(\sqc)$ and MU
gives $-\log_2c$, with $c=\cos^2\frac\theta2$. The two curves cross exactly
once, at $\theta^\ast\approx81.3^\circ$ ($c\approx0.575$), the root of
$h(\cos\frac\theta2)=-2\log_2\cos\frac\theta2$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:entropic} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:entropic}). Nothing singles out this angle geometrically; it is
simply where the two bounds exchange roles. For $\theta<\theta^\ast$, that is,
unless the measurements are close to mutually unbiased, the direct-sum Shannon
bound is the stronger of the two, and it becomes asymptotically tight in ratio
as the measurements approach compatibility, precisely where MU is weakest.
Only in the narrow window $\theta^\ast<\theta\le90^\circ$ next to mutually
unbiased measurements ($\theta=90^\circ$), for which MU is tight, does MU give
the better bound; for the tensor-product bound the crossover lies at
$79.3^\circ$. For the Shannon entropy alone, the closed-form
bound~\eqref{eq:SR} is at least as strong as both at every angle, and
$h(\sqc)$ is never below $78\%$ of the exact minimum, which thus exceeds it by
up to $28\%$; the strength of the majorization bounds lies in their
universality. Of the two forms, the direct sum is
preferable for $\alpha\le1$, and for qubits remains valid up to $\alpha=2$,
while only the tensor product survives for large $\alpha$.

Majorization also makes the distinction between detector noise and detector
bias sharp. Noise acts as a doubly stochastic map on the statistics and can
only increase every measure of uncertainty, whereas bias moves the achievable
set. Whether relations derived for ideal detectors survive is decided by one
partial sum, $S_2$, that is, by $\Lambda\le2\sqc$ for qubits
(Proposition~\ref{prop:detectors}). Uncertainty-based tests of entanglement and
steering with trusted detectors must therefore account for detector bias
explicitly; the closed-form bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:povm} does so for all
Schur-concave measures simultaneously. Non-attainment is itself testable:
experiments have reported Lorenz curves that touch the optimal
bound~\cite{Wang2020}, which by Proposition~\ref{prop:attain} can only happen
for individual partial sums, and the distance
$\min_\rho\max_k[S_k-L_k(p\oplus q)]$ between the bound and the closest state is a
directly measurable quantity. A test with deliberately mismatched detectors,
in the coherent-light~\cite{Wang2020} or photonic~\cite{Yuan2023} settings in
which optimal majorization bounds have already been probed, would probe
Proposition~\ref{prop:detectors} directly.

\emph{Relation to quantum optics.}---These results contrast instructively with
the quadratures of a light mode. There, coherent and squeezed states are
minimum-uncertainty states: for their principal quadratures they saturate both
the Heisenberg relation and the entropic relation of Bia{\l}ynicki-Birula and
Mycielski~\cite{BBM1975}; moreover, all pure Gaussian states are equivalent
under continuous majorization in phase space and are conjectured to majorize
every Wigner-positive state~\cite{VanHerstraeten2023}. For non-commuting binary
measurements no such state exists (Propositions~\ref{prop:attain}
and~\ref{prop:povm-attain}), and the optimal state depends on the uncertainty
measure, in line with the finding of Korzekwa and
Lostaglio~\cite{KorzekwaLostaglio2016} that no qubit state minimizes all
information-theoretic uncertainty measures at once, and with the
measure dependence of qubit uncertainty discussed in
Ref.~\cite{LuisBosykPortesi2016}. The boundary of $\mathcal{E}_\theta$, traced by the pure states in the
plane of the two axes, plays the role of the family of squeezed states, trading
predictability of $N$ for that of $M$. The balanced bisector state, the analogue
of a coherent state, minimizes the Shannon sum up to $\bar\theta\approx67^\circ$;
for larger angles the minimizer moves towards an eigenstate of either
measurement, the analogue of an infinitely squeezed state, and the range in
which the balanced state is optimal shrinks for smaller R\'enyi orders and grows
for larger ones (Sec.~\ref{sec:entropic} and Ref.~\cite{ZozorBosykPortesi2013}).
The ellipse itself resembles the covariance ellipse of a squeezed vacuum only
loosely: it describes the pair of measurements rather than a single state, and
although it is a disk for mutually unbiased measurements and flattens as they
become compatible, its area $\pi\sin\theta$ shrinks instead of being conserved.
A single qubit cannot be squeezed in the usual sense~\cite{KitagawaUeda1993}, as
all its pure states are spin coherent states; the essential difference is
algebraic. For quadratures the commutator is a
$c$-number, which makes the Heisenberg bound state independent. For binary
observables it is the anticommutator that is, blockwise, a $c$-number
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:cov}], and it fixes $c=\frac12+\frac14\|\{A,B\}\|$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:dimd}). The Robertson bound
$\Delta A\,\Delta B\ge|\langle(\mhat\times\nhat)\cdot\vsigma\rangle|$, by
contrast, vanishes for every state whose Bloch vector lies in the plane of the
two axes, which contains all optimal states. Commutator-based relations are
therefore uninformative here, and state-independent descriptions are needed:
tight variance relations~\cite{Abbott2016} or, for all Schur-concave measures
at once, majorization. The information missing from the Robertson bound is
carried by the anticommutator term of the Robertson--Schr\"odinger relation,
$\det\Gamma\ge\langle(\mhat\times\nhat)\cdot\vsigma\rangle^2$ with $\Gamma$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cov}, which every pure state saturates; in the plane of the axes
it reduces to $\det\Gamma=0$, the boundary of $\mathcal{E}_\theta$.

The results apply directly to photonic qubits. Restricted to single photons,
the Stokes operators of polarization are the Pauli matrices, and two linear
polarization analyzers at a relative angle $\vartheta$ correspond to
$\theta=2\vartheta$, so that $c=\cos^2\vartheta$ is Malus's law; the crossover
with MU and the angle $\bar\theta$ correspond to $\vartheta\approx40.7^\circ$ and
$\vartheta\approx33.5^\circ$, respectively. The optimal direct-sum bound has been
tested in this setting with coherent light, the polarization state being
reconstructed from measured Stokes parameters~\cite{Wang2020}. For many photons
the Stokes operators form a collective spin that can be squeezed (polarization
squeezing~\cite{Korolkova2002,Bowen2002}); for a single photon, which cannot be
squeezed, only the trade-off along the boundary of $\mathcal{E}_\theta$
remains. Imperfect detectors lead to the unsharp and biased measurements of
Theorem~\ref{thm:povm}, with the bias set by the mismatch of detection
efficiencies (Sec.~\ref{sec:povm}); by Proposition~\ref{prop:povm-attain}(iii),
such biases can create a trade-off even between two measurements in the same
polarization basis, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:detectors} they can invalidate
the ideal-detector bounds.

Several questions remain open.
(i) \emph{Tensor bounds for POVMs.} The optimal $\omtimes$ requires maximizing
sums of products of affine functions over $\mathcal{E}_\theta$, and we have not
found it for any noisy measurements. For unbiased measurements of equal
sharpness the AM--GM step of Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor} still gives $S_1$, but
$S_3$ is no longer attained at the states that saturate the projective bounds:
numerically, for orthogonal axes and $\eta=\zeta=0.8$ it is attained neither by
an eigenstate nor by a bisector state, and we have not derived it in closed
form. For biased or unequally sharp measurements the AM--GM step itself is no
longer tight, and the least concave majorant in Lemma~\ref{lem:sup} may become
non-trivial.
(ii) \emph{Binary POVMs in dimension $d$.} Corollary~\ref{cor:binary-povm} gives
the optimal direct-sum bound in terms of $\lambda_{\max}(E_i+F_j)$. A closed form
for this eigenvalue, analogous to $1+\|P_iQ_j\|$ for projectors, the conditions
under which the bound is attained, and the tensor-product bound remain open.
(iii) \emph{More measurements.} For qubit bases the eigenvalue formula gives
$S_k=\frac k2+\frac12\max_J\bigl|\sum_{j\in J}\vec n_j\bigr|$, where $J$ runs
over sets of $k$ outcomes and $\vec n_j$ is the Bloch vector of the projector
of outcome $j$, and Li and Qiao obtained the optimal bound for $\sigma_x$,
$\sigma_y$ and $\sigma_z$~\cite{LiQiao2019}. For random triples of bases we
found the increments of $S_k$ always ordered, so that no flattening was needed;
whether this holds in general, and what replaces it for POVMs and for the
tensor product, is open.
(iv) \emph{Mixed orders.} For qubits, conditional majorization bounds yield
optimal relations for any pair of orders~\cite{XiaoFangGour2019}. Whether
state-independent relations of the form~\eqref{eq:maj-UR} can reproduce
mixed-order relations such as $H_\infty(p)+H_{1/2}(q)\ge-\log_2c$ remains
open.
(v) \emph{Binary measurements on light modes.} Which experimentally accessible
binary measurements on a light mode have $c<1$, and what do the bounds of
Sec.~\ref{sec:dimd} imply for them? In infinite dimension
$c=\sup_{i,j}\|P_iQ_j\|^2$ can equal $1$ without a common eigenvector. For
photon-number parity and displaced parity, whose expectation values sample the
Wigner function~\cite{Royer1977}, increasingly squeezed vacua, which contain
only even photon numbers, approach simultaneous eigenstates of both, so no
non-trivial state-independent relation exists. Tests for coherent states,
$\{\ket{\nu}\!\bra{\nu},\mathds{1}-\ket{\nu}\!\bra{\nu}\}$, realized by a
displacement followed by on/off detection with no-click probability
$\pi Q(\nu)=\bra{\nu}\rho\ket{\nu}$, with $Q$ the Husimi function, also have $c=1$,
because all states orthogonal to both coherent states are common eigenvectors.
Individual partial sums remain informative, however:
$\pi[Q(\nu_1)+Q(\nu_2)]\le1+e^{-|\nu_1-\nu_2|^2/2}$, with equality for the
superposition $\ket{\nu_1}+e^{-i\varphi}\ket{\nu_2}$ with
$\varphi=\arg\langle\nu_1|\nu_2\rangle$, an optical cat
state~\cite{Ourjoumtsev2007,Lewenstein2021} (the even cat state for
$\nu_2=-\nu_1$), which thus plays the role of the bisector state. Non-trivial relations require $\|\{A,B\}\|<2$, as for
the pseudospin operators of Ref.~\cite{Chen2002}, or for polarization
measurements on single photons.

\section{Numerical methods}
\label{app:numerics}
% ===========================================================================

All analytical results were also checked numerically. We evaluated Lorenz
curves, the lattice supremum via least concave
majorants~\cite{CicaleseVaccaro2002}, the analytic bounds of
Theorems~\ref{thm:direct}, \ref{thm:tensor} and~\ref{thm:povm} and
Corollaries~\ref{cor:direct}--\ref{cor:tensor}, the eigenvalue characterization
$S_k=\max_{|J|=k}\lambda_{\max}(\sum_{j\in J}K_j)$, and exact minima of R\'enyi
and Tsallis entropy sums.

The closed forms were compared with (a) lattice suprema over $1.8\times10^5$
grid states, for 40 random pairs of qubit bases (Theorems~\ref{thm:direct}
and~\ref{thm:tensor}) and 60 random pairs of qubit POVMs
(Theorem~\ref{thm:povm}), with maximal deviation $<10^{-4}$, and (b) the eigenvalue
characterization for 200 random qubit POVM pairs and for 150 random pairs of
projectors of rank $d/2$ in dimensions $d=2,4,6$, all with $c<1$ (deviation
$<10^{-14}$). Pairs with a common eigenvector, which always occur unless both
ranks equal $d/2$, have $c=1$ and were checked separately; the anticommutator
form of $c$ was checked for 300 random pairs of projectors of arbitrary rank in
dimensions $d\le8$, the tensor-product part of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimd} for 50
random pairs in $d=4,6$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:binary-povm} for 300 random
pairs of binary POVMs in $d=2,\dots,6$. We also checked
Propositions~\ref{prop:attain}--\ref{prop:povm-attain},
Corollaries~\ref{cor:direct}--\ref{cor:tensor},
Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}, the critical angle $\bar\theta$, the motion of the
Shannon minimizer beyond $\bar\theta$, the range of R\'enyi orders in which the
bisector or the eigenstates are optimal, the other values quoted in
Sec.~\ref{sec:entropic}, the covariance form of Lemma~\ref{lem:ellipse}, the
jointly measurable example, the detector model and the behavior of the gap in
Fig.~\ref{fig:povm}(b) of Sec.~\ref{sec:povm}, the
Robertson--Schr\"odinger statement of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}, and the
numerical statements in open questions (i) and (iii), the latter for 300 random
triples of qubit bases. Proposition~\ref{prop:detectors} was checked for 400
random biased POVM pairs (the criterion $\Lambda\le2\sqc$ against the largest
Landau--Pollak sum over $8\times10^3$ states), for 100 random unbiased pairs
(state-by-state majorization) and for the detector models of
Fig.~\ref{fig:detectors}; we also checked the validity of the direct-sum R\'enyi
form up to $\alpha=2$, the order $\alpha_0(c)$, the tensor-product ratio and the
fixed-spectrum bound of Sec.~\ref{sec:povm}. The statements about light modes in
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} were checked in a Fock space of dimension 600.

The exact entropy minima are obtained by a dense grid over the great circle in
$\mathrm{span}\{\mhat,\nhat\}$ refined by Nelder--Mead. For R\'enyi and Tsallis
sums this reduction is justified by the radial argument of
Sec.~\ref{sec:geometry}, which applies equally to unbiased unsharp
measurements. For biased measurements we minimize only the Shannon sum, which
is concave in $(x,y)$ and is therefore minimized on the boundary of
$\mathcal{E}_\theta$ as well. To check that the eigenstates are optimal for
$\alpha\le\frac12$, where the R\'enyi sums have a cusp at the eigenstates, we
parametrize the circle by an angle $\varphi$ and write the outcome probabilities
as $\cos^2(\varphi/2)$ and $\sin^2(\varphi/2)$, which avoids the loss of
precision in $1-\cos\varphi$.
